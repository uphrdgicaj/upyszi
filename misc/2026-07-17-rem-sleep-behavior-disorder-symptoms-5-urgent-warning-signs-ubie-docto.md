REM Sleep Behavior Disorder (RBD) is a sleep condition where people physically act out vivid dreams during the REM stage of sleep. Common symptoms include talking, shouting, punching, kicking, or sudden jerking movements while asleep. Unlike typical dreaming, the body's normal muscle paralysis during REM sleep is absent, allowing dreamers to move freely — which can lead to injuries to themselves or their bed partners. RBD is more common in older adults and may be an early warning sign of neurological conditions like Parkinson's disease.

If you or a loved one are experiencing symptoms like these, understanding the cause is the critical first step. Sleep disorders can overlap with other conditions, making self-diagnosis difficult and potentially delaying important care. REM Sleep Behavior Disorder, or RBD, is a condition where people act out their dreams during the REM stage of sleep. This can include making loud noises, talking, yelling, or even getting out of bed and moving around. Sometimes, these movements can be violent, and people may accidentally hurt themselves or their bed partners.
