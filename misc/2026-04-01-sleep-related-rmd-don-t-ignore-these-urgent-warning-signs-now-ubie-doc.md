What is Sleep-Related Rhythmic Movement Disorder (RMD)?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Sleep-Related Rhythmic Movement Disorder (RMD) is a sleep condition characterized by repetitive, rhythmic motions such as head banging, body rocking, or leg rolling that occur just before or during sleep. While RMD is most commonly seen in infants and young children—often resolving on its own by age 5—it can also persist into or appear in adulthood. Episodes typically last less than 15 minutes and may involve humming or vocal sounds. Though usually harmless, severe cases can disrupt sleep quality or cause injury, warranting evaluation.

If you or your child are experiencing repetitive movements during sleep, it's important to understand whether the symptoms point to RMD or another underlying sleep or neurological condition. Getting clarity early can help you take the right next steps—whether that's reassurance, lifestyle adjustments, or a conversation with a specialist.
