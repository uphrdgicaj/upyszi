# 5 Types of Skin Hyperpigmentation — and How a Dermatologist Picks the Right Treatment for Each | Ubie Doctor's Note

5 Types of Skin Hyperpigmentation — and How a Dermatologist Picks the Right Treatment for Each

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Skin hyperpigmentation falls into five main types: post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH), melasma, solar lentigines (sun spots), freckles, and drug-induced discoloration. Each has distinct causes—including acne, hormones, UV exposure, and certain medications—and each requires a different treatment approach.

Dermatologists commonly treat hyperpigmentation with topical lightening agents such as hydroquinone or retinoids, chemical peels, laser therapy, or medication adjustments. The best option depends on pigmentation depth, root cause, and skin type.

Because these five types often look alike but respond very differently to treatment, correctly identifying your specific type is the essential first step. Using the wrong product can deepen discoloration or delay healing. Explanation

Hyperpigmentation skin issues—those darker patches, spots or streaks—are common. They happen when your body produces extra melanin, the pigment that gives skin its color. While these patches are usually harmless, they can affect self-confidence. Understanding the type of hyperpigmentation you have is the first step toward choosing the right treatment.

Below we cover five common forms of hyperpigmentation, what causes each one, and how a dermatologist decides on the best approach. What it isDark spots or patches left behind after skin inflammation or injury—such as acne, eczema, bug bites or minor cuts.

Why it happens

Prevention & Ongoing Care

No matter which type of hyperpigmentation skin concern you have, good daily habits help prevent new spots:

Wear broad-spectrum sunscreen (SPF 30 or higher) every day

Reapply sunscreen every two hours when outdoors

Seek shade and wear protective clothing, hats and sunglasses

Use gentle cleansers and avoid harsh scrubbing or picking at your skin

When to Seek Medical Advice

Most hyperpigmentation skin conditions are harmless, but it's important to watch for:

Rapidly changing, asymmetric or bleeding spots

Spots with irregular borders or multiple colors

Any new dark patches accompanied by itching, pain or swelling
