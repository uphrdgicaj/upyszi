Your doctor breaks down the science, benefits, and practical tips for using NMN for skin rejuvenation.

What Is NMN and Why It Matters

Nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) is a naturally occurring molecule in our bodies. It's a direct precursor to nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+), a coenzyme that:

What Research Tells Us

While much of the NMN data comes from preclinical studies, emerging human trials paint a promising picture:

Preclinical (Animal) Studies

A 2020 mouse study found NMN supplementation improved skin thickness and reduced signs of photoaging.

NMN-fed mice showed higher collagen levels and better skin elasticity.

Human Studies

Early phase clinical trials confirm that oral NMN safely raises blood NAD+ levels in healthy adults.

Ongoing research is examining direct skin outcomes like wrinkle reduction, hydration, and texture improvements.

Takeaway: The foundational science supports the idea that NMN for skin rejuvenation is plausible, but long-term, large-scale human studies are still in progress.

Commonly studied doses range from 250–500 mg daily.

Some protocols go up to 1,000 mg, but evidence for added benefit and safety beyond 500 mg is still emerging.

Timing

NMN is often taken in the morning to align with natural metabolic rhythms.

Splitting doses (e.g., morning and mid-day) may help maintain steady NAD+ levels.

Product Quality

Choose pharmaceutical-grade, third-party tested NMN supplements.

Verify purity (≥99%) and avoid products with fillers or additives.

Possible Side Effects

Most users report minimal to no side effects.

Drug Interactions

NMN is generally well tolerated, but always check with your doctor if you're on prescription medications.

NMN works best as part of a holistic skin-health plan. Consider pairing it with:

Together, these lifestyle factors support the cellular improvements NMN delivers.
