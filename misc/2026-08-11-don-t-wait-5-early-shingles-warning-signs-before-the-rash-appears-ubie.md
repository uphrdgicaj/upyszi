What are the first warning signs of shingles before the rash appears?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Shingles often begins with warning signs before any rash appears. In the days leading up to a shingles outbreak, you may notice pain, tingling, burning, or itching in a specific area on one side of your body or face. Some people also experience numbness, sensitivity to touch, or a general feeling of being unwell. These early symptoms typically occur in a localized band or patch, since shingles follows the path of a single nerve.

Explanation

Shingles, also known as herpes zoster, often starts with symptoms that occur before the rash shows up. According to medical literature, the early signs can include pain, tingling, or itching in a specific area on one side of your body or face. This is because the virus affects the nerves in that area.

Early Symptoms of Shingles:

Pain: This is one of the most common initial signs. The pain can be sharp, burning, or throbbing.

Tingling or Itching: You might feel a tingling or itchy feeling, which can be uncomfortable.

Sensitivity: The skin in the affected area may become very sensitive to touch.

These symptoms are caused by the virus affecting the nerves, leading to what is known as neuralgia. If you're experiencing unusual pain, tingling, or sensitivity on one side of your body, try our free AI symptom checker to help identify what might be causing your symptoms. It's important to recognize these early signs because they can help in diagnosing and starting treatment for shingles before the rash appears. Early treatment can help manage symptoms better and may prevent complications.
