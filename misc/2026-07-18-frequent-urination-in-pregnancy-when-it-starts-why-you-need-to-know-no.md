Published on: 7/15/2026

At what stage of pregnancy does frequent urination typically begin?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Frequent urination is a common early pregnancy symptom that can persist throughout all three trimesters. It's typically caused by rising hCG hormone levels, increased blood volume, and later, pressure from the growing baby on the bladder.

Why it happens:

First trimester: Hormonal shifts (especially hCG) increase blood flow to the pelvic area and kidneys.

Second trimester: Symptoms may ease slightly as the uterus rises out of the pelvis.

Third trimester: The baby's weight presses directly on the bladder, often causing urgent, frequent trips to the bathroom.

Frequent urination is a common symptom during pregnancy. It often starts in the first trimester and can continue throughout the pregnancy. This happens because the body is going through many changes.

Why It Happens:

In Early Pregnancy: The body produces more blood, and the kidneys have to process more fluid, leading to more trips to the bathroom.

In Later Pregnancy: As the baby grows, it puts pressure on the bladder, making you feel like you need to urinate more often.

Other Factors:

Hormonal Changes: Hormones can affect how often you need to urinate.

Gestational Diabetes: This condition can also lead to more frequent urination.

Understanding these changes can help you know what to expect during pregnancy.
