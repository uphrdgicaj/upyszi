What are NMN and NAD+?

NMN stands for “nicotinamide mononucleotide.” It’s a precursor to a coenzyme in the body called nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+). Your body converts NMN into NAD+, which, in turn, can enhance the efficiency with which your body’s cells function.5 Some studies show that it also enhances athletic performance by increasing your VO2 max after a short period of use.6 Others connect it with improved insulin resistance, resulting in lower diabetes risk and, potentially, a trimmer waistline.7

How NMN becomes NAD+

NMN’s conversion to NAD+ takes place in the salvage pathway, one of three metabolic systems that generate NAD+ in the body. The other two systems, the Preiss-Handler and de novo synthesis pathways, rely on dietary sources of vitamin B3 and tryptophan, respectively. But the salvage pathway can use external sources like vitamin B or the NAD+ precursors NR and NMN while also being able to recycle NAM back into the salvage pathway when it's released as a byproduct of NAD+ usage.29

That's why the salvage pathway is sometimes referred to as an NAD+ recycling system: NAM converts to NMN and then to NAD+, and when your body uses NAD+, it releases NAM that the salvage pathway can use again.

Here’s a look at the salvage pathway in action:

Balance is critical in the salvage pathway, especially concerning methyl donors, which allow NAM to convert into NMN on its way to becoming NAD+.29 Since NAD+ utilization leaves behind NAM as a byproduct, if you ramp up NAD+ production, you end up with an overabundance of NAM without enough methyl donors to sustain the newly increased conversion rate.29 30 That can give other actors, like NNMT, license to convert unclaimed NAM to MNAM, introducing a potential source of cellular harm — the opposite of what you want.36

Some NMN products contain additional methyl donors to support increased NAD+ production. Trimethylglycine (TMG) is among the most effective and widely used in NAD+ supplements, and Innerbody Labs is tied with a few other companies for having the highest dose (500mg) in its class.

When the salvage pathway is well-managed, NMN supplementation can result in some noteworthy health benefits. Let’s take a slightly deeper look at how NMN supplementation has been shown to improve health.

Potential benefits of NMN supplementation

Boosting NAD+ levels is at the core of NMN’s potential to improve health and wellness, but some NMN-specific research has revealed certain secondary effects not seen from other NAD+ precursors. Whether these are ultimately attributable to NAD+ levels or NMN itself is still under investigation, but the results are important to investigate.

Here are a few of the ways NMN has improved health parameters for participants — beyond increasing NAD+ levels — in prominent research:

Sleep quality

A 12-week-long study in more than 100 adults found that just 250mg of NMN per day could improve sleep quality, reduce fatigue, and boost physical performance.31 Typically, 250mg is the lowest dose available in commercial NMN supplements, and it’s the per-capsule dose in Neurogan’s NMN 500.

Cardiovascular fitness

A study in amateur runners gave participants NMN doses of 300mg, 600mg, or 1,200mg per day. After six weeks, runners in the 600mg and 1,200mg groups showed increased VO2 max (peak oxygen consumption capacity), increased oxygen uptake, and peak power at first and second ventilatory thresholds, all indicating improvements in aerobic capacity from NMN.6 Many NMN supplements have suggested doses around 500mg, but some push further, with Neurogan providing 900mg in its NMN 900 and Innerbody Labs providing 700mg in its NAD+ Support.

Blood pressure

Another 12-week 250mg NMN study looked at pulse wave velocity as a measure of arterial stiffness in 36 middle-aged adults. It found that supplementation resulted in decreased stiffness, which is associated with improved blood pressure and cardiovascular health.32

Skin quality

A study in liver transplant patients with actinic keratosis (a patchy, flaky skin disorder) found that a daily NMN dose of 500mg could dramatically reduce symptoms for nearly all of the participants, with more than half seeing a complete remission of the disorder.33 Neurogan NMN 500 provides this dose in its two-capsule serving.

DNA protection

A study in mice and humans from 2021 points toward NMN’s potential to protect DNA from degradation during replication. Specifically, it revealed that short-term NMN supplementation at 300mg/day could increase telomere length (the protective caps on the ends of DNA strands), reducing the risk of aging and age-related disease.34 Most NMN supplements on the market provide at least that much, making it an easy target to hit if your primary goal is longevity.

Nicotinamide riboside (NR) is another NAD+ precursor that boasts a fair amount of convincing scientific evidence supporting its effectiveness.28 While most research looks at one precursor or another, and most companies provide one or the other in their supplements, the choice between the two might not be entirely necessary.

If we were pressed to make a recommendation between the two, we’d likely say that NMN’s larger body of human research and closer metabolic proximity to NAD+ make it the superior choice, but both will effectively raise NAD+ levels.

To make the choice yourself, you might want to ask yourself if there are specific goals you have in mind beyond longevity and increased NAD+ levels. For example, if you were determined that your NAD+ precursor should also improve brain health and protect against neurodegenerative disease, you’d want NR. How an NMN supplement works

Compared to NAD+, NMN is a smaller molecule and can more easily pass through cell membranes. That makes it far superior to direct supplementation with NAD+, which the body has difficulty absorbing.

When you take NMN, a transporter on the cell’s surface invites it in, where it can convert to NAD+ in the liver within 15 minutes. Studies using intramuscular injection of NMN showed its ability to create NAD+ in various organs and systems.5

NAD+ production is dose-dependent, which means that the more NMN you consume, the more NAD+ you’ll produce — up to a certain limit. As we mentioned above, there could be long-term safety concerns with high NMN doses. There are cancer risks that haven’t been fully explored, as well as the potential for the salvage pathway to stall if you don’t supply it with methyl donors to support an increased NAM-to-NAD+ conversion rate, resulting in MNAM accumulation and increased risk of fat accumulation and insulin resistance.9 36 It’s up to you to balance your risk-to-reward ratio with a given dose.

How NMN supplements absorb

The supplements you’ll see in this guide come in various forms, which can significantly impact their effectiveness. One form of medicine will absorb more or less quickly than another, and it depends mainly on how the company compounds its drugs and the mechanism by which they’re delivered.

Currently, most NMN research has used capsules as the delivery mechanism, so there’s too little data on other delivery methods as they pertain to NMN. However, NMN is similar to a vitamin in many respects, and other mechanisms for vitamin delivery have been studied extensively. These include studies of liposomal nanocarriers as well as intranasal, sublingual, and transdermal delivery methods.

While sublingual delivery may be faster-acting, it doesn’t seem to increase long-term bioavailability, at least according to one vitamin B study.13 And lipid nanocarriers can theoretically protect NMN until it gets to the small intestine, where a dedicated NMN transporter is waiting to help you absorb it, but there’s more than one type of liposomal delivery system, and each will act differently in delivering specific elements or compounds.14 To date, the only liposomal NMN study that’s been published was extremely small (just 15 participants) and resulted in a modest 25% increase in NMN efficiency.46

So, for those of you more interested in using cutting-edge delivery mechanisms with the highest potential for fast and thorough absorption, novel methods like sublingual or intranasal delivery or liposomal encapsulation might be enticing. But if you want to go with what’s been shown to be both safe and effective, standard encapsulation should be your choice at this time.15

Common ingredients seen alongside NMN supplements

NMN has a fair amount of research supporting its potential to improve various biomarkers on its own, but numerous supplement makers have seen fit to combine it with other ingredients. In some cases, these ingredients serve to support healthy NAD+ production, while, in others, their presence is more related to specific goals like anti-aging.

Let’s take a closer look at some of these extra ingredients you’re likely to find within our top picks:

Trimethylglycine (TMG, or betaine)

TMG is a useful addition to the salvage pathway in that it acts as a methyl donor, helping to fuel the conversion of NAM into NMN on its way to becoming NAD+.29 This is crucial because increasing NAD+ production will also increase NAD+ utilization and breakdown, and one of the byproducts of NAD+ breakdown is NAM. That NAM is typically recycled back into the salvage pathway by using more methyl donors to become NMN again, and then NAD+. But if there’s a shortage of methyl donors, actors such as N-nicotinamide methyltransferase (NNMT) can come in and hijack that NAM, converting it into potentially harmful compounds like MNAM.18 36 TMG also has the potential to improve cardiovascular health and athletic performance on its own, albeit at doses higher than those used in most NMN and NR supplements.39

Spermidine

Spermidine has a slightly less direct role to play in NAD+ metabolism compared to TMG, but it also works against the potential dangers posed by NNMT.37 As a polyamine, its presence balances against NNMT, suppressing its production and protecting NAM from getting diverted out of the salvage pathway.

Like TMG, spermidine also has a host of its own longevity benefits, including an undeniable connection between higher spermidine intake and increased lifespan.38

Black pepper fruit extract

Black pepper fruit extract, often listed as piperine or the brand-name ingredient BioPerine, is well-known in the supplement industry for its ability to increase nutrient absorption.27 In NAD+ precursor research, it’s been shown to increase NAD+ concentrations following NMN supplementation by about 15%.40

Resveratrol

A couple of the companies that we profile here use resveratrol in some of their NMN supplements. That's because resveratrol has repeatedly been shown to activate sirtuin 1 (SIRT1), and NAD+ and sirtuin activity go hand-in-hand — the former being dependent on the latter for regulating cellular energy and signaling pathways associated with aging.19 And resveratrol has repeatedly been shown to activate sirtuin 1 (SIRT1).20 It also boasts cardioprotective, neuroprotective, and cancer-fighting benefits.11 So, by combining NMN with resveratrol, you can theoretically increase SIRT1 and improve on its potential by doing so in the presence of additional NAD+.21 However, these are theoretical connections between resveratrol’s potential and NAD+ metabolism; research confirming their relationship is still developing.

There have been some significant side effects documented in resveratrol studies, including nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and even liver problems.22 But these issues arose in studies that looked at doses up to 100 times higher than what you often find in NMN supplements.11 Prior studies have revealed that resveratrol’s positive and negative actions in the body are dose-dependent.12

Ultimately, if you’re considering an NMN supplement with resveratrol, you should speak with your doctor to make sure that you’re a good candidate and that the brand in question uses a dose that’s appropriate for you.

Quercetin and fisetin

Quercetin and fisetin are popular anti-aging supplements that may have the ability to help clear out senescent cells — cells that have stopped dividing and whose waste material can contribute to age-related disease. Quercetin boasts the better research history of the two, though its most prominent study also looks at it in conjunction with other known senolytics.41 Most other research into these ingredients’ anti-aging potential has been conducted in animals. It’s promising, but further human research is needed, and a direct relationship to NAD+ has not been established.

Currently, one problem in NAD+ data in general seems to be a lack of research into long-term usage related to cancer. There’s no evidence to suggest that NMN or elevated NAD+ levels can cause cancer. Still, there’s some evidence that NAD+’s ability to increase cellular efficiency also applies to cancer cells. Researchers successfully inhibited tumor growth by reducing those cells’ access to NAD+.9 This implies that an abundance of NAD+ might fuel preexisting or developing cancer in the body. But at the same time, there’s contradictory research that shows NAD+’s protective potential against certain other types of cancer.10

So, both of these things may be true:

NAD+ has anti-aging effects that may involve protecting against the development of cancer.

NAD+ could make already-developing cancers worse.

We need more data to conclude whether elevated NAD+ levels pose a cancer risk. At the moment, we know too little about NAD+’s entire metabolic lifespan and its effects in the body’s various systems to narrow down which of those two things is more characteristic of NAD+. Scientists may eventually identify an ideal dose of NMN or NR — or find a quick, accurate, and inexpensive way to measure and maintain a Goldilocks NAD+ level in the body — but for now, it’s essential to be aware of the unknowns. Talking to your doctor or nutritionist is always a smart move when considering a new supplement.

Alternatives to NMN

The word “longevity” gets bandied about quite a bit by companies selling NMN and similar products. There’s some evidence that increasing NAD+ levels can offset some of the common markers associated with aging, but enduring the passage of time is an incredibly complex ordeal that pills can’t solve. (At least, not yet.)

Longevity wellness is an industry unto itself, with NMN just one of many avenues for exploration. Let’s take a quick look at some other supplements and approaches you can take to enhance your lifespan, either by lengthening it or improving the time you have. Some of these can be utilized in conjunction with NMN or each other, but there may also be some instances in which taking too many anti-aging products can have the opposite effect. Be sure to discuss your longevity goals with a doctor and develop a comprehensive and safe approach fit for your needs.

Nicotinamide riboside (NR)

NR is the closest comparable supplement to NMN. Both are precursors to NAD+, but they each act a little differently in the body. NR has to turn into NMN before it can increase NAD+. It was previously thought that NMN couldn’t get into cells the way NR could, rendering it mostly useless. New discoveries have shown NMN’s ability to get inside cells and start producing NAD+, but it’s possible that one may work better for you than another.48 Some companies, like Innerbody Labs and Renue by Science, sell supplements that combine NR and NMN to increase your odds of success.

NAD+ injections

Some companies offer NAD+ injections that you can perform at home. These deliver NAD+ directly to your bloodstream, with no precursor required. However, most research into this kind of administration is performed via IV infusion, not through single subcutaneous doses, which is the method of at-home injection.50

Resveratrol is one of the most popular and widely researched antioxidants on the market.12 It’s been shown to have anti-cancer and anti-inflammatory properties, as well as neuro- and cardioprotective benefits.11 Like NMN and NR, there’s some controversy regarding its relationship to tumorigenesis, but the scientific picture is likewise incomplete.

Multivitamins

Multivitamins offer a litany of helpful vitamins, minerals, amino acids, and botanicals that can help the body function more efficiently. Finding the right blend for you is a challenge, especially given the tremendous variety out there, but we have dedicated guides that can help.

Diet and exercise

Nobody wants to hear this one, but there’s undeniable truth to it. Pizza, beer, and donuts are delicious, but they won’t help you live longer. If you’re serious about improving the length and quality of your life, you’ll want to enjoy such foods in moderation and get as much greenery, lean protein, and healthy fats as you can. And you don’t have to run a half-marathon every day to see the benefits of exercise. Resistance training has been shown to increase NAD+ levels, and even just walking a bit after meals can make a big difference to your health.49 24

NMN supplement FAQ

Do NMN supplements work?

[NMN Supplement](https://copperglow.dns0663.com/blog/8846865880.html)

Based on the available research and our testing, NMN supplements are effective at elevating NAD+ levels in the body. Multiple studies show increased NAD+ levels, while others connect NMN with improved athletic performance, sleep, blood sugar, and more.1 23 There are some caveats regarding absorption, but you can expect some degree of effectiveness from most of your options.

Are NMN supplements dangerous?

For short-term use, there’s little evidence that NMN supplements are dangerous.8 Unfortunately, as with many other supplemental ingredients, there are also currently no studies looking at long-term NMN usage or the long-term effects of elevated NAD+ levels.4 Some evidence suggests that a high NAD+ level not only fights aging but may also strengthen cancer cells at the same time as it strengthens noncancerous cells.25 More research is needed to clarify the issue. Those with cancer, recovering from cancer, or with a family history of cancer may want to wait until the science is clearer before trying NMN. Talking with your doctor is an important part of evaluating whether NMN is safe for you.

What’s the best dose for NMN?

The best dose for NMN will be different for everyone. It’s also hard to calculate the exact differences in absorption among NMN’s various delivery methods. We recommend starting at a lower dose — 500mg or less — and working your way up. It may be wise to purchase a 500mg dose with a serving size of two pills (250mg each) and only take one pill for the first few weeks. That will be a smart way to test its efficacy while saving you some money.

How should I take NMN (capsules, powders, liquid, etc.)?

While sublingual powders and liquids or even nasal sprays theoretically get around NMN’s middling bioavailability in the GI tract, the fact remains that nearly 100% of NMN research has been conducted using plain capsules containing a given dose for that study. Without research looking into these other delivery methods specifically for use with NMN, there’s no way to formulate a dose that could reliably be safe and effective. You could end up with something that simply doesn’t work or something that drastically increases your side effect risk, with no way to know how much of a dosage adjustment to make to improve your experience.

As such, capsules are the best way to take NMN or any other NAD+ booster until more research can verify safe and effective dosing for these alternative methods.

Is NMN better than NR?

NMN has not been shown to be superior or inferior to nicotinamide riboside (NR). Still, NR has to turn into NMN in the body before it can increase NAD. One relatively recent study showed that exogenous NMN can cross your cell membranes with greater ease than previously thought.48 Studies also show more side effects associated with NR than NMN, including muscle cramps and digestive upset.35
