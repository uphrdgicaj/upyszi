Myasthenia gravis is diagnosed through a combination of tests, including blood tests to detect specific antibodies (such as anti-AChR or anti-MuSK), nerve conduction studies like repetitive nerve stimulation and single-fiber electromyography (EMG), and a thorough physical exam assessing muscle weakness and fatigability. Imaging, such as a CT or MRI of the chest, may also be used to check for thymus abnormalities.

Because myasthenia gravis shares symptoms with many other neurological and autoimmune conditions, identifying the cause of muscle weakness early is critical to getting the right treatment.
