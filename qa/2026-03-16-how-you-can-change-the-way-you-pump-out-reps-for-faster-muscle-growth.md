# How You Can Change the Way You Pump Out Reps for Faster Muscle Growth

Jeremy Ethier, kinesiologist, fitness trainer, and founder of Built with Science, is back with another video to help you achieve faster muscle growth. He previously shared his top exercise to add to your warmup for faster gains. Now, he's addressing how the way you rep out your exercises can lead to more muscle growth in a shorter amount of time.

"One of the biggest areas of confusion for lifters is how exactly you should execute the reps of your exercises in order to elicit the most muscle growth from those reps," says Ethier. "Should you do low reps, high reps, heavy weight, light weight, do slow reps, or do fast reps?"

Ethier believes the best way to get the most bang for your buck is to execute your reps with these three rules.

How to Lift to Maximize Growth

Rule 1: Stick mostly to a hypertrophy rep range of roughly 6 to 12 reps for each of your sets. Rule 2: Use moderately heavy weight or resistance so you can stay in that 6 to 12 rep range. Rule 3: Use a lifting tempo that is around 1 second on the way up and 1 second on the way down for each rep.

"These three 'rules' will, for the most part, help you build lean muscle faster," says Ethier.

However, focusing on tension needs to be your priority.

According to Ethier, there are two steps you should start applying right away to maximize your tension.

Step 1: Mastering execution."Because your body is designed to cheat, it's going to try to find the easiest way out during your reps. You may be lifting heavier or doing more reps, but you’re not actually providing more tension to your target muscle, and hence won’t experience the growth," says Ethier. "Take a look at your exercises, and see if you’ve been compromising your form during your reps."

If that's the case, Ethier suggests lightening the weight you're using or decreasing the reps so you can execute with precision and optimal form.

Step 2: Apply internal focus."Applying internal focus is the idea that we can stimulate and grow our target muscles to an even greater degree by shifting more of our focus to them working during each rep," says Ethier. "Your main goal should not be to simply do your required number of reps and sets for that day."

[Out Reps for Faster](https://mendstride.snaketongueofficial.com/blog/1140963672.html)

Instead, he says that you you need to be mindful and actively use your target muscle. You should think about your muscles contracting with each rep instead of focusing on just getting the weight up or doing a certain number of reps.

"Applying this will inevitably result in your exercises now becoming considerably more challenging. It can be tempting to stop using this internal focus purely in order to achieve your target number of reps or hit a certain amount of weight for that day," says Ethier. "But these extra reps and weight just do more harm than good, because that extra tension isn’t being directed towards growing your target muscle. Put your ego aside and realize that this is indeed the fastest way to gain muscle (instead of mindless reps)."

However, there is an exception to this for heavier sets of lower reps and compound lifts like deadlifts or squats. Instead, Ethier says you should instead focus your attention on execution and simply getting the weight up. You'll be much safer that way, too.

The bottom line: you need to realize why you’re doing all of the exercises in your workout.

"If your aim is to use those exercises to stimulate your muscles to grow them, then you’re going to get far better results in the long run by prioritizing your execution and focusing on the muscles you’re supposed to be working as opposed to just moving weight from point A to point B," says Ethier. In other words, if you're not trying to become the strongest guy in the gym, stop worrying about how much you can do. Instead, focus on how you do it.
