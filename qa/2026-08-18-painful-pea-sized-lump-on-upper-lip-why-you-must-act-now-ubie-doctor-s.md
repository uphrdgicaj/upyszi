A painful, pea-sized lump on the upper lip is most commonly caused by a blocked salivary gland (mucocele), a benign tumor, a cyst, or an infected hair follicle. Less often, it may signal an oral infection, an allergic reaction, or, rarely, a malignant growth. Location, texture, and duration all help narrow the cause.

A painful lump in the upper lip can arise from several causes:

Sialolithiasis (Salivary Gland Stone): This condition occurs when a stone forms in a salivary gland, leading to pain and swelling. The lump might feel firm and can be painful, especially during eating when the gland tries to produce saliva.

Benign Tumors: Conditions like glomangioma or angiomyoama can present as painful lumps in the lip area. Glomangioma is a rare vascular tumor that can cause discomfort, while angiomyoama is another benign growth associated with pain.

Other Conditions: There are various other possibilities, including infections, cysts, or even inflammation of the tissues in the lip.
