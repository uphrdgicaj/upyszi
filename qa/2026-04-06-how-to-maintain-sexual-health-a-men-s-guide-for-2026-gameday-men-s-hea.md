Maintain a Healthy Weight and Metabolic Balance

Excess body fat directly impacts sexual health. Fat tissue increases the conversion of testosterone into estrogen, which can lower libido, reduce energy, and make erections less reliable. (4) Weight gain is also closely tied to insulin resistance, higher blood sugar levels, and chronic inflammation.

Metabolic dysfunction is one of the most common underlying contributors to erectile dysfunction, and some studies show that men with metabolic syndrome are almost twice as likely to suffer from ED than those without it. (5)

When blood sugar and insulin are poorly controlled, blood vessels become less responsive and circulation suffers. Over time, this makes it harder to achieve and maintain erections.

Reduce Stress and Improve Mental Well Being

Mental health has a powerful influence on sexual performance. Chronic stress raises cortisol levels, which suppress testosterone production and interfere with arousal. Anxiety can disrupt the brain-body connection required for erections, even when physical health is otherwise in good condition. (7)

Performance anxiety is also common and can often be self-reinforcing. One negative sexual experience can create negative anticipation and increase anxiety, which can lead to future performance issues. Relationship stress, unresolved conflict, and lack of communication can also contribute to difficulties in the bedroom.

While some mental health issues require substantial changes and professional treatment, many sexual and performance-based anxieties can be solved by simple practices like breathwork, meditation, setting boundaries, lowering expectations, and creating stress-free environments.

Improve Sleep Quality for Better Libido and Performance

Sleep is one of the most overlooked pillars of sexual health. The majority of daily testosterone production occurs during deep sleeping hours. When sleep is short or fragmented, hormone levels suffer. (8)

Poor sleep also increases cortisol levels, worsens insulin resistance, and raises the risk of erectile dysfunction. Men who regularly sleep fewer than six hours per night are far more likely to report low libido and performance issues and may see as much as a 10 to 15% drop in serum testosterone levels. (9)

Men should typically aim for 7-9 hours of sleep per night. Consistent bed and wake times, limiting late night screen exposure, and keeping the bedroom cool and dark all support deeper sleep. Improving sleep quality often leads to noticeable improvements in energy, mood, and sexual performance within weeks.

Eat Right for Hormonal and Sexual Health

Nutrition directly affects hormones, circulation, and metabolic function. A diet of healthy, nutrient-rich foods provide the building blocks needed for healthy testosterone production.

Adequate protein supports muscle mass and hormone synthesis. Healthy fats from sources like olive oil, nuts, seeds, and fatty fish are essential for hormone production. Micronutrients such as zinc, magnesium, and vitamin D also play important roles in testosterone levels and erectile function.

Highly-processed foods, excess sugar, and frequent refined carbohydrates contribute to insulin resistance and inflammation. Hydration is also important, as dehydration can impair circulation and energy levels. While supplements that claim to improve testosterone production are widely marketed, most men benefit more from improving dietary quality first and using targeted supplementation only when deficiencies are identified.

Limit Alcohol, Stop Smoking, and Reduce Toxins

Alcohol and nicotine both have well documented effects on sexual health. Regular alcohol use lowers testosterone and interferes with nerve signaling involved in erections. While occasional drinking is unlikely to cause long-term harm, consistent heavy use increases the risk of erectile dysfunction. (10)

Smoking, meanwhile, damages blood vessels and reduces nitric oxide availability, which is essential for erections. (11) Even vaping exposes blood vessels to substances that impair function over time. Recreational drug use can also affect libido, hormone levels, and performance.

Reducing exposure to environmental toxins where possible is also a smart way to reduce barriers to sexual performance. Plastics, endocrine-disrupting chemicals, and poor air quality may sometimes contribute to hormonal imbalances.

Keep Up With Routine Medical Evaluations

Sexual health is closely linked to overall health for men. Conditions like high blood pressure, high cholesterol, and diabetes often show early warning signs through erectile dysfunction, but may be a sign of a larger medical issue.
