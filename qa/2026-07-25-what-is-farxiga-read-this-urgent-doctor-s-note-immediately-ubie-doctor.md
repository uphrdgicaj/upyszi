# What is Farxiga? Read This Urgent Doctor's Note Immediately | Ubie Doctor's Note

What is farxiga?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Farxiga (dapagliflozin) is a prescription SGLT2 inhibitor that lowers blood sugar by helping the kidneys remove excess glucose through urine. It is FDA-approved to treat type 2 diabetes, reduce hospitalization risk in heart failure (particularly with reduced ejection fraction), and slow the progression of chronic kidney disease.

Key considerations include who should avoid Farxiga, potential side effects such as genital yeast infections, dehydration, and rare diabetic ketoacidosis, along with proper dosing, kidney function monitoring, and interactions with diuretics or insulin.

Explanation

What Is Farxiga?

Farxiga (generic name dapagliflozin) is a prescription medication used to help manage certain chronic conditions. It belongs to the class of drugs known as SGLT2 inhibitors (sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 inhibitors). Farxiga works by helping your kidneys remove excess glucose (sugar) from your bloodstream through your urine.

Approved Uses of Farxiga

Type 2 diabetes mellitus• Helps lower blood sugar when diet and exercise alone aren't enough• Often used with other glucose-lowering medicines

Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF)• Reduces risk of hospitalization for heart failure• Can improve symptoms like shortness of breath and fatigue

Chronic kidney disease (CKD)• Slows kidney function decline• May delay need for dialysis

How Farxiga Works

Inhibits the SGLT2 protein in the kidney's proximal tubules

Prevents reabsorption of filtered glucose

Increases urinary glucose excretion

Reduces blood pressure mildly

Promotes weight loss through calorie loss in urine

Benefits of Farxiga

Blood Sugar Control• A1C reduction of about 0.5–1.0% when added to standard care (McKeage & Perry, 2019)

Dosage and Administration

Standard starting dose: 10 mg once daily, in the morning, with or without food

For type 2 diabetes, lower doses (5 mg) may be considered in older adults or those with moderate kidney impairment

Swallow tablets whole; do not crush or chew

Always follow your doctor's instructions exactly. Do not adjust your dose or stop Farxiga without consulting your healthcare provider.

Potential Side Effects

Common side effects (occur in 2–10% of patients):

Urinary tract infections (UTIs)

Genital yeast infections in men and women

Increased urination

Thirst

Less common but serious side effects:

Dehydration, leading to low blood pressure, dizziness or fainting

Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), a rare but serious condition even with near-normal blood sugar

Acute kidney injury

If you experience severe abdominal pain, trouble breathing, rapid heartbeat, or persistent vomiting, seek medical attention immediately.

Warnings and Precautions

Kidney Function: Farxiga is less effective if your estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) is below 45 mL/min/1.73 m². In severe kidney impairment, doctors may withhold the drug.

Liver Disease: Patients with decompensated cirrhosis (advanced liver disease with fluid buildup) were excluded from major trials. According to EASL guidelines, medications affecting fluid balance should be used cautiously in these cases. Always inform your doctor about any history of liver problems.

Blood Pressure: Can cause a modest drop in blood pressure. If you're on diuretics or have low baseline blood pressure, monitor yourself for dizziness.

Electrolyte Imbalances: Monitor sodium and potassium levels, especially if you take diuretics.

Who Should Avoid Farxiga

Type 1 diabetes (high risk of DKA)

History of severe genital infections or recurring UTIs

Severe kidney impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²)

Lifestyle Tips to Enhance Farxiga's Effects

Stick to a balanced diet low in simple sugars and refined carbs

Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate exercise weekly

Maintain adequate hydration, especially in hot weather or during exercise

Limit alcohol, as it can increase dehydration and hypoglycemia risk

When to Seek Medical Help

Signs of serious infection: fever, unusual genital pain or swelling

Symptoms of DKA: nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, rapid breathing, confusion

Severe dizziness, fainting spells

Sudden decrease in urine output or dark-colored urine

Final Thoughts

Farxiga offers significant benefits for blood sugar control, heart failure management, and kidney protection. However, it's not right for everyone. Discuss your full medical history—including kidney or liver disease—and all medications you take with your doctor before starting Farxiga. If you experience any serious or life-threatening symptoms, seek immediate medical attention.

Speak to a doctor before making any changes to your treatment plan or if you have concerns about Farxiga's risks versus benefits. Your healthcare provider can help tailor therapy to your individual needs.
