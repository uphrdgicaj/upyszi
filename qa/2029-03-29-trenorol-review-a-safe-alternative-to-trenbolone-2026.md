Is Trenorol safe?

Trenorol should be safe for most healthy adults. However, we recommend consulting your doctor or dietician before beginning any new supplement to ensure it will work for you, your diet, and your fitness regimen. This is even more imperative if you suffer from any health conditions — particularly autoimmune issues — or are currently taking medication like blood thinners.

Let’s take a look at potential health risks associated with each ingredient:

β-sitosterol

β-sitosterol can cause GI discomfort and diarrhea in healthy individuals. Plant sterols, in general, have been studied for their positive influence on the immune system and as anti-inflammatory agents.13 But that elevated immune response can work against those with certain autoimmune disorders.

Samento inner bark

Samento inner bark poses a serious risk for miscarriage at high doses, as well as potentially dangerous immune responses in people with autoimmune diseases. It can also act as an anticoagulant, making it dangerous for those on blood thinners.14

Nettle leaf extract

Nettle has known anticoagulant effects. People on blood thinners should avoid nettle leaf extract, as the two can combine to cause exaggerated bruising or prevent sufficient wound healing.15

Pepsin

Pepsin is normally made in the body, and exogenous pepsin appears to be safe in doses commonly used to aid in digestion. However, it can cause GI problems such as abdominal pain and diarrhea, and there is a small risk of skin rash as a reaction.16

Obviously, compared to steroids, these ingredients are extremely safe, but compared to other similar workout supplements, we’d say they’re moderately safe. Comparable pre-workouts from companies like Transparent Labs, Animal, or Swolverine have variegated enough ingredients that they each come with specific risk factors. However, Trenorol’s use of multiple ingredients with anticoagulant properties creates a particular danger for one group — those on blood thinners — who might do better looking into other supplements.

If you notice anything akin to an adverse reaction — even if you got your doctor’s approval to take Trenorol — you should stop taking it for a while to see how you feel and report any noteworthy reactions to your doctor.

Pre-workouts

Trenorol’s dosing guidelines advise you to take the supplement just before a workout and to take it in the morning on any off days. That schedule makes it closer to a pre-workout than anything else, and it warrants a look at other available pre-workout supplements to ensure that Trenorol is your best option.

A pre-workout is essentially any supplement designed to have its effects set in shortly after consumption and for those effects to give you added strength, stamina, or focus during bouts of exercise. Common pre-workout ingredients include:

Caffeine

Caffeine is well-known for its ability to improve alertness, energy, and athletic performance.24 It’s especially useful in a pre-workout if you exercise in the morning. But if you workout later in the evening or you’re sensitive to caffeine, you should seek out non-stimulant pre-workouts.

Creatine

Research suggests that creatine supplementation can help prevent ATP depletion during workouts, providing more cellular energy during anaerobic exercise.25

Carnitine

Carnitine appears capable of enhancing bloodflow and oxygen supply to the muscles during exercise, and it may protect against exercise- or age-related muscle loss.26

Arginine

Arginine is often associated with nitric oxide mediation, causing vasodilation and improving antioxidant action that can improve athletic performance and recovery.27

Citrulline

Citrulline predominantly serves as a precursor to arginine, increasing the amount of serum arginine in the blood and providing the same results.28

Taurine

An analysis of ten scientific papers studying the effects of taurine on athletic performance found increased lipid metabolism and decreased oxidative stress during and after workouts.29 However, the average dose used in these studies was far higher than what’s typically found in supplements.

BCAAs

Branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs) can help reduce muscle soreness in resistance-trained individuals,30 but claims of increased muscle synthesis require more research.31

B Vitamins

The body uses B vitamins in important energy pathways and as a critical part of tissue repair — like the kind your muscles need after a workout.32

Many people may prefer pre-workouts to Trenorol simply thanks to the addition of caffeine.
