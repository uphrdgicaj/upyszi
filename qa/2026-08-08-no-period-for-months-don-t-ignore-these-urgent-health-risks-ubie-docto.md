Missing your period for several months, a condition known as secondary amenorrhea, can sometimes be normal. Common causes include stress, significant weight changes, intense exercise, hormonal shifts, pregnancy, or approaching menopause. However, prolonged absence of menstruation—especially when paired with symptoms like fatigue, hair loss, acne, headaches, or pelvic pain—may signal an underlying issue such as PCOS, thyroid dysfunction, or hormonal imbalance that deserves medical attention.

Because the causes range from harmless lifestyle factors to conditions requiring treatment, understanding what's driving your missed periods is the first step toward getting answers. Explanation

When you miss your period for several months, this is known as amenorrhea. Sometimes, your body might stop having periods because of stress, significant weight loss, or too much exercise. This is called functional hypothalamic amenorrhea and is often temporary and not a sign of a serious problem. On the other hand, stopping menstruation can also be a signal of other hormonal changes or conditions. It is important to think about any changes in your body, like changes in weight, energy levels, or other symptoms you might be experiencing. If you have missed several periods and cannot explain why, or if you notice other changes like unexpected weight gain or loss, unusual hair growth, or discomfort, you can use a free symptom checker to help identify possible causes before consulting with a doctor. They can check your hormone levels and overall health to make sure everything is working as it should. Remember, while it can be normal under certain circumstances, a prolonged absence of periods should be evaluated to rule out any underlying issues.
