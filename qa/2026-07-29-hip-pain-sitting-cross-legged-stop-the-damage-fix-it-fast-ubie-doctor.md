Hip pain when sitting cross-legged is often caused by tight muscles like the piriformis, hip flexors, or glutes, as well as added stress placed on the hip joint and surrounding structures. Common contributors include poor flexibility, muscle imbalances, prolonged sitting, or underlying issues like bursitis or hip impingement. Gentle stretching, adjusting your sitting posture, and using cushions or supports can often ease discomfort.

Explanation

When you sit cross-legged on the floor, it puts extra pressure on your hip joint and the muscles around it, especially the piriformis muscle. This position may also strain your sacroiliac joints, which connect your spine to your pelvis. The pressure and stretch in these areas can cause pain if your muscles are tight or not used to that position.

To help reduce the pain, try doing gentle stretching exercises for your hips and thighs before sitting in that position. You can also change how you sit—for example, placing a soft cushion or folded towel under your hips may help spread out the pressure more evenly. Taking breaks to stand or move around can also relieve discomfort.
